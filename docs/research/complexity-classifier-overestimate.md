## 1. Problem

The Brick production classifier (`qwen35-complexity-lora`, LoRA on Qwen3.5-0.8B
trained on 65K cognitive queries) **systematically overestimates** query
difficulty. The router sends too many queries to large models, increasing
operating costs without a proportional benefit in quality.

### Initial measurement

On 2K MMLU-Pro (canonical benchmark) with cognitive labels:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Classifier accuracy | 34.2% |
| **Overestimate rate** | **50.5%** |
| Underestimate rate | 15.3% |
| End-to-end routing accuracy | 83.45% (1669/2000 correct) |
| Avg cost/query | 8.90 (units) |

Every second query is classified into a higher tier than necessary. On the full
12K MMLU-Pro with **empirical labels** (derived from the 3 real models in our
stack) overestimation rises to **61.5%**.

---

## 2. Statistical framing: the Gaussian Shift

### Hypothesis

The classifier's prediction distribution is a Gaussian bell curve **shifted to
the right** relative to the ideal distribution. As small-tier model capabilities
grow (Qwen3.5-9B today ≈ GPT-4-0613 from 2023), the ideal routing threshold
progressively moves to the left. Historical 2023 MMLU-Pro labels are now stale.

### Quantification

Comparison of production predictions vs empirical labels derived from the 3
models of our real stack on 11,657 queries:

| Distribution | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Empirical (ground truth)** | **75.6%** | **9.3%** | 15.1% |
| **Predicted by classifier** | 19.4% | 66.6% | 14.1% |
| **Δ** | **−56 pt** | **+57 pt** | −1 pt |

Shift metrics:

- **KL(pred ‖ emp) = 1.05 bits**
- **Wasserstein = 0.58 tiers** (each query on average 0.58 tiers too high)

### By category

| Category | Overestimate | KL |
|---|---|---|
| chemistry | 79.6% | **2.42** (worst) |
| biology | 69.2% | 1.85 |
| economics | 67.7% | 1.79 |
| math | 63.7% | 1.43 |
| law | 54.9% | **0.44** (best) |

STEM is worse because empirically most STEM questions are easy (all 3 models
answer them correctly) but the classifier sees formulas and technical terms and
promotes them to medium.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Training dataset evolution

We tested two label sources:

#### A. Empirical labels (9K) — derived from 3 real models

```text
n_correct == 3  →  easy    (all 3 correct)
n_correct == 2  →  medium  (2/3 correct)
n_correct <= 1  →  hard    (at least 2 wrong)
```text

Distribution: 75% easy, 9% medium, 15% hard. **Too skewed**: the medium class
has only 891 training examples, insufficient to learn medium/hard recall.

**B. LLM-judged labels (76K) — `train_llm_judged_122b.jsonl`**

Labels generated by Qwen3.5-122B as LLM-judge on 76K queries, on the same model
present in the routing stack. More balanced distribution:

- medium: 51% (39K)
- easy: 33% (25K)
- hard: 16% (12K)

Dataset **8x larger** and well balanced. First choice for production training.

### 3.2 Asymmetric loss

Standard cross-entropy penalizes all errors equally. To minimize overestimation,
we introduce an **asymmetric cost matrix**:

```text
over_cost[true, pred] =
  [0, 3, 9]     # true=easy:   costs 3x to predict medium, 9x to predict hard
  [0, 0, 3]     # true=medium: costs 3x to predict hard
  [0, 0, 0]     # true=hard:   no overestimation possible
```text

Total loss:

```text
L = CE_with_soft_target(logits, smoothed_target[true])
    + λ · E_pred[over_cost[true, pred]]
```text

### 3.3 Asymmetric label smoothing

Target distribution not one-hot but:

```text
true=easy   → [0.92, 0.08, 0.04]
true=medium → [0.08, 0.84, 0.08]
true=hard   → [0.04, 0.08, 0.92]
```text

Asymmetric choice for `true=hard` (more mass on medium than on easy) discourages
the model from being overconfident.

### 3.4 Custom validation metric

Early stopping not on `eval_loss` (generic) but on:

```text
combined = macro_F1 − 1.5 × overestimate_rate
```text

It incorporates the economic objective directly into the training stopping criterion.

### 3.5 Training setup

- **Base:** `Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B` (fresh, no continued fine-tuning)
- **System prompt:** identical to production (drop-in compatibility)
- **LoRA:** r=32, alpha=32, dropout=0.1, target q/k/v/o/gate/up/down
- **Optimizer:** AdamW, lr=1e-4 cosine, warmup 6%, weight_decay=0.01
- **Epochs:** 3 with early stopping patience=2-3
- **Batch:** 16, bf16, max_length=768
- **Sampler:** WeightedRandomSampler inverse-freq for class balance

## 4. Systematic ablation of variants

We trained 8 variants to map the hyperparameter space. All numbers are
**end-to-end routing** on the 2000-query benchmark:

| Variant | Base | Dataset | over_λ | Smoothing | Acc | Cost | cost/acc | Medium recall (e2e) | Hard recall (e2e) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Production** (baseline) | 0.8B | 65K cognitive | 0 (standard CE) | none | **83.45%** | 8.90 | 10.66 | 86.9% | 60.1% |
| eco v1 | 0.8B | 9K empirical | 0.7 | asymm 0.08 | 81.35% | **2.51** | **3.08** | 71.6% | 60.8% |
| eco v2 | 0.8B | 9K empirical | 0.5 + med_boost | asymm 0.08 | 81.50% | 2.73 | 3.35 | 75.4% | 59.2% |
| mid v1 | 0.8B | 9K empirical | 0.3 | asymm 0.08 | 81.25% | 3.16 | 3.89 | 69.6% | 61.6% |
| mid v2 | 0.8B | 9K empirical | 0.2 + med_boost=2 | symm 0.05 | 81.25% | 3.16 | 3.89 | 69.6% | 61.6% |
| pro v1 | 0.8B | 9K empirical | 0 | asymm 0.05 | 80.65% | 2.69 | 3.33 | 76.4% | 57.4% |
| pro v2 | 0.8B | 9K empirical | 0 + med_boost=2.5 + hard_boost=3 | symm 0.02 | 80.65% | 2.69 | 3.33 | 76.4% | 57.4% |
| eco-2B | **2B (VL)** | 9K empirical | 0.7 | asymm 0.08 | 80.20% | **1.24** | **1.55** | — (collapsed always-easy) | — |
| **judged-asym** | 0.8B | **76K LLM-judged** | 0.7 | asymm 0.08 | **82.40%** | 8.06 | 9.78 | **87.8%** | 61.1% |
| Oracle | — | — | — | — | 89.65% | 3.37 | 3.76 | 100% | 13.4%* |

*Oracle hard: 13.4% because 206 queries are solved by none of the stack (→
routed hard but still fail anyway).

### 4.1 Lessons from the variants

1. **over_lambda is marginal between 0.3 and 0.7**: eco v1/v2 and mid are almost
   indistinguishable. The real lever is dataset+smoothing, not lambda.

2. **Empirical 9K is not enough for medium/hard recall**: dataset too small with
   medium=891 examples → model learns "easy or nothing". Empirical medium recall
   → 10.5% in training, then recovers to 70% end-to-end because easy queries
   misclassified as medium are often solved by 122B.

3. **2B VL collapses with over_λ=0.7**: the larger capacity finds the degenerate
   "always easy" minimum that the 0.8B does not reach. Useful only as an
   "eco-extreme" variant (cost/acc 1.55).

4. **LLM-judged 76K is the game-changer**: jump from macro_f1 0.42 → **0.77**
   (+82% improvement), medium recall 10.5% → 68.6%, hard recall 22.5% → 71.6%.
   8x more data with coherent labels from Qwen3.5-122B.

### 4.2 Eco vs Judged vs Production

Direct comparison of deployable candidates:

| Metric | Production | brick-eco | **brick-judged** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2K correct** | **1669/2000** (83.45%) | 1627/2000 (81.35%) | 1648/2000 (82.40%) |
| Avg cost/query | 8.90 | **2.51** | 8.06 |
| cost/acc | 10.66 | **3.08** | 9.78 |
| Classifier acc (on 2K cognitive) | 34.2% | 61.15% | 40.05% |
| Classifier macro F1 | low | 0.42 | **0.77** |
| Medium recall (e2e) | 86.9% | 71.6% | **87.8%** |
| Routing distribution (E/M/H) | 402/1315/283 | 1734/141/125 | 621/1037/342 |

**brick-judged is the best classifier overall** (macro F1 0.77) but end-to-end
routing also counts cost: very close to production on accuracy (−1pt) with 9%
cost saving.

**brick-eco** optimizes for cost: 72% saving vs production at a cost of 2pt accuracy.

## 5. "Impossible queries" analysis

Of the 2000 benchmark queries, **207 (10.3%) are not solved by any of the 3
models** in the stack (9B, 122B, MiniMax). Category distribution:

| Category | Impossible queries |
|---|---|
| law | 46 |
| other | 19 |
| psychology | 18 |
| engineering | 17 |
| chemistry | 16 |
| health | 16 |

Test with **MiniMax-M2.5 with reasoning_effort=high**: on 60/207 completed
retries, **9 correct (~15%)**. High reasoning recovers cases outside the normal
budget but the large majority (85%) remains impossible. These 207 queries
represent the **structural ceiling** of the stack: Oracle cannot exceed
1793/2000.

## 6. Final product line

### Simplified proposal: **eco + pro**

| Variant | Model | Correct on 2K | End-to-end Accuracy | Avg Cost | cost/acc | Story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **eco** | brick-eco (0.8B + asymm 9K empirical) | 1627 | **81.35%** | **2.51** | **3.08** | Max saving (−72% cost) |
| **pro** | production `qwen35-complexity-lora` | **1669** | **83.45%** | 8.90 | 10.66 | Max accuracy |

Gap: **42 queries of difference** (2.1pt), with **3.5x cost ratio** in favor of eco.

### Alternative for 3 tiers (if needed)

Adding **mid = brick-judged** (1648 correct, 82.40% acc, cost 8.06) between eco
and pro:

- eco → mid: +1.05 pt acc, cost 3.2x
- mid → pro: +1.05 pt acc, cost +10%

Coherent story but the mid→pro gap is narrow. Better eco+pro.

### Discarded experimental variants

- **2B VL eco**: cost/acc 1.55 (the best overall) but collapsed to always-easy,
  zero medium/hard recall. Useful only as an "eco-extreme" use case.
- **mid v1/v2 / pro v1/v2 (empirical 9K)**: all in the 80.65–81.50% range, cost
  2.6–3.2. Insufficient spread to differentiate the product.
- **Multi-tier regression**: abandoned — it would have introduced specificity to
  the 3 models in the stack, compromising generic portability of the classifier.

## 7. Economic interpretation

### 7.1 Operating cost per 1M queries

Assuming baseline cost per query at the easy tier = €0.001:

| Strategy | Cost per 1M queries (€) | Correct/1M |
|---|---|---|
| always medium | 10,000 | 862,500 |
| Production (pro) | 8,900 | 834,500 |
| **Brick-judged (mid)** | **8,060** | **824,000** |
| always easy | 1,000 | 803,000 |
| **Brick-eco** | **2,510** | **813,500** |
| Oracle | 3,370 | 896,500 |

**eco vs pro**: saving **€6,390 per million queries**, at a cost of ~21K fewer
correct answers (2.1% loss).

### 7.2 Break-even

The eco vs pro trade-off pays off if: *cost of a wrong answer < €0.30 / query*.

In cases where a routing wrong answer ≠ a totally wrong answer (the easy model
still answers, with lower quality), eco is dominant.

## 8. Conclusions

1. **The Gaussian shift is real and quantified**: production overestimates 61.5%
   of queries on the full 12K empirical set. Average 0.58 tiers above the
   correct one.

2. **LLM-judged 76K is the best fresh ground truth**: 8x larger than empirical
   9K, more coherent labels, enables high medium/hard recall (68–72%).

3. **Asymmetric loss + smoothing + custom validation metric** are the 3 key
   levers. Applied on the right dataset (76K) they produce the best classifier
   (macro F1 0.77).

4. **over_lambda is insensitive** between 0.3 and 0.7. The real lever is the
   dataset, not lambda tuning.

5. **Capacity is not enough**: 2B VL with the same loss collapses. The problem
   was not 0.8B capacity but signal quality.

6. **Very favorable eco vs pro trade-off**: eco costs 72% less at a cost of 2pt
   end-to-end accuracy. Per 1M queries: €6.4K saving.

7. **Periodic recalibration** needed: the ideal Gaussian moves as models evolve.
   The labeling pipeline (LLM-judge with the current stack) + asymmetric
   training is reusable for each iteration.
